Huize Frankendael is the last country estate of Amsterdam. Founded in 1660, privately inhabited for many years, and open to the public since 2008 — as a wedding venue, a place for gatherings and an exhibition space.

The house stands in the middle of Park Frankendael, with a symmetrical formal garden behind it and eighteenth-century salons, boardrooms and the coach house within. Restaurant Merkelbach (opens in a new tab) occupies the right coach house, open seven days a week.

Restaurant Merkelbach

Restaurant Merkelbach carries the name of city architect Ben Merkelbach, who lived here with his family from 1957 to 2004. The kitchen fully endorses Slow Food; in the formal garden, home to the terrace, you experience the seasons within the city.

Open seven days a week for coffee & cake, à la carte lunch and drinks. Reservations are handled by the restaurant itself.

Three centuries of history

Frankendael is the only remaining country estate in the Watergraafsmeer. Izaak Balde gave the house its current appearance around 1733, with rococo stuccowork inside and later a classicist gate by Jacob Otten Husly on the Middenweg. Read the full history.
